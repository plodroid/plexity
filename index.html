ORDER FLOW · SIMPLE MODE

Sweep or continuation?

Use SVP for location, CVD for aggression, and the footprint for execution detail. The key question is simple: after price takes liquidity, does the market reject the new area or accept it?

continuation evidence sweep / reversal evidence context
SVPWhere?Location / value
CVDWho?Aggressive side
FootprintHow?Execution detail
THE CORE IDEA

Effort vs result

If aggressive buyers or sellers put in huge effort but price barely moves, someone may be absorbing them. If aggression and price move together, continuation is healthier.

ABSORPTION

Big effort, little progress

Strong delta+Price stalls=Possible trap

Example: CVD rips higher at the prior high, but the candle rejects and closes back below it. Buyers were aggressive, yet price could not hold higher.

ACCEPTANCE

Big effort, real progress

Strong delta+Price expands=Continuation

Example: price breaks the prior high, CVD rises with it, footprints show buy imbalances, and pullbacks hold above the breakout.

DECISION FLOW

5 questions after the sweep

1Did price accept outside the level or immediately come back inside?
2Did CVD confirm the move, or diverge from price?
3Does the footprint show real buy/sell imbalances?
4Is there absorption at the extreme: huge aggression with little price progress?
5Does the next pullback hold the breakout or reclaim the old range?
Sweep / reversal evidence Level taken → aggression spikes → price fails → reclaim

Think: “they hit hard, but got nowhere.”

Continuation evidence Level breaks → aggression confirms → price expands → retest holds

Think: “they hit hard, and price actually followed.”

Important: this is confirmation, not certainty. CVD and footprints can differ by data feed and venue, especially outside centralized futures markets. Use them with the level, session context and risk management—not as a standalone signal.
